BASIC LEVEL

1. Scheduling & To-Do Lists

Part A: Simple Scheduling

Most students fail because they schedule Results ("Finish Chapter 5") instead of Time Allocations ("Study Chap 5: 2pm-4pm").

The System: Use Fixed vs. Flexible blocks. Fixed blocks are non-negotiable (classes, sleep). Flexible blocks are for deep work.

Part B: The MIT To-Do List

Do not write 20 items. You will fail. Write 3 MITs (Most Important Tasks) per day.

Rule: If you do nothing else but these 3, the day is a success. This combats "Execution Bias" (doing easy things to feel busy).

MIT 1: Past Paper (Heavy)
MIT 2: Physics Derivation (Medium)
MIT 3: Vocab Review (Light)
...Everything else is a bonus.
MEDIUM LEVEL

2. The Eisenhower Matrix & Batching

Part A: Decision Architecture (Eisenhower)

Separate URGENT from IMPORTANT. Most urgency is noise.

  • Q1: Do First. Urgent + Important (Exam tomorrow).
  • Q2: Schedule. Not Urgent + Important (Exercise, Long-term study). *The Winner's Zone*
  • Q3: Delegate. Urgent + Not Important (Emails, Interruptions).
  • Q4: Delete. Neither. (Doomscrolling).

Part B: Task Batching

Context Switching Cost: It takes ~23 minutes to regain focus after interruption. Stop multitasking.

Strategy: Group similar tasks. Answer all emails at once. Do all math problems at once.

ADVANCED LEVEL

3. Habit Stacking & Energy Management

Part A: Habit Stacking

Don't rely on willpower. Rely on triggers. Use the formula:

"After I [Current Habit], I will [New Habit]."

Part B: Energy Mapping

Time is static. Energy is dynamic. Are you a Morning Lark or Night Owl?

Golden Rule: Protect your peak energy hours for your hardest task (The "One Thing"). Do admin/chores in low energy troughs.

EXPERT LEVEL

4. Strategic Architecture & Life Balance

Long Term Systems

Expert students plan in layers: Annual (Direction) → Monthly (Milestones) → Weekly (Execution).

The Weekly Review: Sunday night is for "Architecture". Design the week. If it's not on the calendar, it doesn't exist.

Sustainable Productivity

You are a biological system, not a machine. You need recovery. "Burnout is not a badge of honor; it is a system failure."
