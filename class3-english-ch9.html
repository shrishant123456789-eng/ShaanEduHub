Chapter 9: Writing Skills – Basic

📝 Chapter Notes

1. Writing Letters Correctly

Definition: Forming capital and small letters of the alphabet with proper shape and size.

We write letters on four lines. The top line is the headline, middle is waistline, bottom is baseline, and there is a dotted line between. Capital letters touch top and bottom lines. Small letters stay mostly between baseline and waistline.

Capital A: Slant line down, slant line down, middle line across.
Small a: Circle on the right, line down to baseline.
💡 Memory Tip: Tall letters touch the sky (top line), short letters stay on the ground!
[Image: Four-line writing pattern with letter examples]

Quick Check

Where do capital letters touch? (Top and bottom/Bottom only)

2. Writing Words Neatly

Definition: Joining letters together with proper spacing to form readable words.

Letters in a word sit on the baseline. Leave small finger spaces between letters. Do not make letters too big or too small. All letters in a word should be the same size (except capitals at start). Write slowly and carefully!

Good: C-a-t (equal size, proper space)
Bad: C a t (too much space) or CAT (all big capitals in middle)
💡 Memory Tip: Finger Space! Use your finger to measure space between words!
[Image: Neat handwriting vs messy handwriting comparison]

Quick Check

How much space between letters in a word? (One finger space/Lots of space)

3. Writing Simple Sentences

Definition: Writing groups of words that make complete sense with a capital start and full stop end.

A sentence starts with a capital letter. It has a subject (who) and a verb (action). It ends with a full stop (.), question mark (?), or exclamation (!). First write slowly, then you can write faster when neat.

Examples:
• The cat sleeps.
• I love my mother.
• This is a pen.
💡 Memory Tip: Sentence = Capital + Words + Sense + Stop (.)!
[Image: Simple sentence structure with capital and full stop]

Quick Check

A sentence must start with _______ letter.

4. Picture-Based Sentence Writing

Definition: Looking at a picture and writing a sentence about what you see.

Look at the picture carefully. Find the main person or thing (subject). Find what they are doing (action). Write: The (thing) is (action). Or: (Name) (action) (object). Match your words to the picture!

Picture: Boy kicking ball
Sentence: The boy is playing football.
Or: He kicks the ball.
💡 Memory Tip: See it, Say it, Write it! Look, Think, Write!
[Image: Picture of children playing with space to write sentence]

Quick Check

What do we look for first in a picture? (Who is doing what/Colors)

5. Completing Sentences

Definition: Filling in the missing part of a sentence to make it complete and meaningful.

We get a sentence with a blank (___.). We must write the missing word. Look at the other words to understand what is missing. It could be a name, action, or thing. Make sure the sentence makes sense!

Complete: I eat _____.
Answer: I eat rice. / I eat mango.
Complete: _____ is my friend.
Answer: Ram is my friend.
💡 Memory Tip: Fill the hole to make it whole! Complete the thought!
[Image: Incomplete sentence with blank filling activity]

Quick Check

Complete: The cat _____. (sleeps/runs)

6. Sentence Expansion

Definition: Making a short sentence longer and better by adding more details.

Small sentence: The boy runs. Big sentence: The tall boy runs fast in the park. We can add: How? (fast, slowly), Where? (in park, at school), When? (morning, evening), Which? (the red ball, the big dog).

Short: I eat.
Expanded: I eat rice daily in the morning.
Add: What? (rice), When? (daily, morning)
💡 Memory Tip: Make it FAT! Add details to make it thick and full!
[Image: Small sentence growing bigger with added words]

Quick Check

Expand: She sings. (Add where)

7. Use of Correct Punctuation

Definition: Using marks like full stop, question mark, and comma correctly in writing.

Full stop (.) ends a sentence. Question mark (?) for questions. Comma (,) for small pause between words. Capital letter after full stop. Apostrophe (') for ownership (Ravi's book).

(.) I am a boy. (Statement)
(?) Are you ready? (Question)
(,) I bought apples, mangoes, and bananas. (List)
💡 Memory Tip: STOP for full stop, ASK for question mark, REST for comma!
[Image: Punctuation marks with examples]

Quick Check

Which mark ends a question? (? / .)

8. Writing with Correct Spelling

Definition: Writing words with all correct letters in the right order.

Spell words correctly by sounding them out (phonics). If unsure, look at the word wall or dictionary. Practice difficult words many times. Do not skip letters or add extra letters. Write neatly so you can see your spelling!

Correct: School, Friend, Mother, Father
Wrong: Skool, Frend, Mothr, Fathr
Tip: Sound it out: M-o-t-h-e-r
💡 Memory Tip: Look, Cover, Write, Check! Look at word, hide it, write, then check!
[Image: Spelling practice with correct and incorrect examples]

Quick Check

🎯 Important Questions

1. What are the four lines used for writing?

Answer: Headline, waistline, baseline, and dotted midline.
These help in placing letters correctly.

2. Where do capital letters touch?

Answer: Top line (headline) and bottom line (baseline).
Capital letters are tall from top to bottom.

3. What is sentence expansion?

Answer: Making a short sentence longer by adding details.
We add words to give more information.

4. Name three punctuation marks.

Answer: Full stop (.), Question mark (?), Comma (,)
These help us understand how to read sentences.

5. What mark ends a question?

Answer: Question mark (?)
We use ? when asking something.

6. What is finger space?

Answer: The space of one finger kept between words when writing.
It helps separate words clearly.

7. Give one way to check spelling.

Answer: Look, Cover, Write, Check method.
Look at word, hide it, write from memory, verify.

8. What letter starts a sentence?

Answer: Capital letter.
Every sentence must begin with a capital letter.

9. Expand: The dog sleeps.

Answer: The brown dog sleeps under the tree.
Added color (brown) and place (under tree).

10. What is picture-based writing?

Answer: Writing sentences describing what we see in a picture.
Observing picture details and writing about them.

11. Complete: I drink _____.

Answer: Water / Milk / Juice (any suitable)
Any logical completion is correct.

12. Why is neat handwriting important?

Answer: So others can read it easily.
Neat writing is readable and looks good.

13. Where do small letters mostly stay?

Answer: Between baseline and waistline.
Small letters are shorter than capitals.

14. What is a full stop used for?

Answer: To end a sentence.
It shows the sentence is complete.

15. Correct the spelling: FREND

Answer: FRIEND
I comes before E in friend.

16. Write a sentence about a cat.

Answer: The cat is sleeping. / The cat drinks milk.
Any correct sentence with proper punctuation.

17. What do we add to expand sentences?

Answer: Details like color, size, place, time.
Adjectives and adverbs give more information.

18. Which is correct: tHe or The?

Answer: The
Only first letter capital unless it's a name.

19. What is completing a sentence?

Answer: Filling in the missing word to make sense.
Making incomplete sentences meaningful.

20. How many fingers space between words?

Answer: One finger space.
Not too much, not too little - just right.

21. Spell: SCHOOL

Answer: S-C-H-O-O-L
C and H together make 'ch' sound.

22. What does comma (,) indicate?

Answer: A small pause or break in sentence.
Used in lists or where we pause while reading.

23. Write a question sentence.

Answer: What is your name? / How are you?
Must end with question mark.

24. What is phonics?

Answer: Sounding out letters to spell words.
Connecting sounds with letters.

25. Arrange: morning / I / in / school / go / the / to

Answer: I go to school in the morning.
Proper word order makes correct sentence.

26. Write short form of "do not".

Answer: don't
Apostrophe replaces the missing letter o.

27. Correct: i like ice cream

Answer: I like ice cream.
Capital I and full stop needed.

28. What is writing?

Answer: Putting letters together to make words and sentences on paper.
Written expression of language.

29. Expand: Birds fly.

Answer: Birds fly high in the sky.
Added how (high) and where (in sky).

30. Write one rule for good handwriting.

Answer: Sit straight / Hold pencil properly / Write slowly.
Any valid handwriting rule is correct.

  • Capital letters touch top and bottom lines

  • One finger space between words

  • Full stop (.) for statements
  • Question mark (?) for questions
  • Comma (,) for pauses and lists
  • Picture writing: See it, Say it, Write it
  • Complete sentences by filling logical words
  • Expand sentences by adding details (who, what, where, when)
  • Correct spelling: Look, Cover, Write, Check
  • Neat handwriting is readable handwriting
  • Every sentence starts with capital letter
  • Phonics helps with spelling (sound-letter connection)
  • Apostrophe for contractions (don't, can't)
  • Consistent letter size improves writing
  • Practice makes handwriting perfect
  • Sit properly while writing
  • Read what you write to check mistakes
