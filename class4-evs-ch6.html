📖 Chapter Notes

1. Types of Houses

A house protects us from heat, cold, rain, wind, thieves, and wild animals. We all need shelter to live safely.

🖼️ Drawing of Kutcha vs Pucca House
  • Kutcha Houses: Made of mud, straw, wood, and bamboo. Found mostly in villages. They are temporary. Example: Huts.
  • Pucca Houses: Made of bricks, cement, steel, and glass. Found in cities. They are strong and permanent. Example: Apartments, Bungalows.
Special Houses
  • Igloo: A house made of snow blocks. Found in very cold places (Arctic). Used by Eskimos.
  • Houseboat: A floating house on water. Found in Kashmir and Kerala.
  • Stilt House: Built on tall wooden poles (stilts) in places with heavy rain and floods (like Assam).
  • Tent: Made of canvas or strong cloth. Used by campers, soldiers, and nomads. Easy to move.
🇮🇳 India Example: In Assam, it rains a lot, so people build Stilt houses to keep water out!
🧠 Tip: 'Kutcha' sounds like 'Kaccha' (Weak/Raw). 'Pucca' sounds like 'Pakka' (Strong/Sure).
2. Materials Used

Different materials are used to build different parts of a house.

  • Walls: Bricks, Stones, Cement.
  • Floor: Marble, Tiles, Cement, Mud (in villages).
  • Doors/Windows: Wood, Glass, Steel.
  • Roof: Tiles, Tin sheets, Concrete (flat roofs), Straw (huts).
3. Houses & Climate
🖼️ Drawing of Flat Roof vs Sloping Roof
  • Hot Regions (Rajasthan): Houses have thick walls to keep heat out and flat roofs.
  • Cold/Rainy Regions (Hills): Houses have sloping roofs so snow and rain slide off easily.
  • Flood Prone Areas: Stilt houses keep the house dry above water level.
4. Clothing & Fibres

We wear clothes to cover our body and protect ourselves from weather and insects.

Fibres are thin threads used to make cloth. Two types:

  1. Natural Fibres: Obtained from plants or animals.
    • Cotton (Plant) - For Summer.
    • Wool (Sheep) - For Winter.
    • Silk (Silkworm) - For Parties (Shiny).
    • Jute (Plant) - For bags/ropes.
  2. Synthetic (Man-made) Fibres: Made in factories using chemicals.
    • Nylon, Polyester, Rayon.
    • They are strong, dry quickly, but catch fire easily.
Clothes for Seasons
  • Summer: Light cotton clothes. They absorb sweat and keep us cool. Light colors reflect heat.
  • Winter: Dark colored woollen clothes. They trap body heat and keep us warm.
  • Rainy: Raincoats and gumboots (made of rubber/plastic) over normal clothes to stay dry.
5. Safety & Sustainability
♻️ 3 Rs: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle!
  • Electrical Safety: Never touch switches with wet hands. Cover sockets.
  • Fire Safety: Don't play with matches. Synthetic clothes catch fire fast (be careful in kitchen).
  • Surroundings: Do not let water stagnate (mosquitoes breed). Keep drains covered.
  • Sustainable Use: Use materials that don't harm nature. Recycle paper and glass. Reuse plastic bottles.

⭐ Important Questions

Q1. Why do houses in hills have sloping roofs?
Ans: So that snow and rain can slide off easily and not collect on the roof.

Q2. Why do we wear cotton clothes in summer?
Ans: Cotton is light and airy. It absorbs sweat from our body and keeps us cool.

Q3. What is a synthetic fibre? Give examples.
Ans: Fibres made by humans in factories are synthetic. Examples: Nylon, Polyester.

Q4. How is a Pucca house different from a Kutcha house?
Ans: Pucca houses are permanent and made of brick/cement. Kutcha houses are temporary and made of mud/straw.

Q5. Why should we not wear nylon clothes while cooking?
Ans: Because synthetic fibres like nylon catch fire very easily and can melt on the skin.

Q6. What materials are used to make an Igloo?
Ans: Blocks of hard snow (ice) are used to make an Igloo.

Q7. Define 'Stilt House'.
Ans: A house built on high bamboo poles to stay safe from floodwater is called a stilt house.

Q8. From where do we get wool?
Ans: We get wool mainly from the hair (fleece) of sheep.

Q9. Why are dark-colored clothes preferred in winter?
Ans: Dark colors absorb more heat from the sun and keep us warm.

Q10. What is a caravan?
Ans: A house on wheels (like a van or bus) that can move from place to place.
