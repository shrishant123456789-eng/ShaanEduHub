1. Visual Perception & Color

BASIC

Design isn't just about "looking good"; it's about communication. We start with how the brain processes visuals.

Memory Tip: The brain processes images 60,000x faster than text. In India, Red = Celebration/Danger, White = Peace/Mourning. Context is key!

Color Models

  • RGB (Red Green Blue): Used for screens (Insta, YouTube). Light-additive.
  • CMYK (Cyan Magenta Yellow Key): Used for printing (Posters, Billboards). Ink-subtractive.

Color Psychology

  • Blue: Trust (Paytm, SBI, Facebook).
  • Red: Urgency/Hunger (Zomato, Netflix, YouTube).
  • Yellow: Optimism/Caution (Bumble, Construction).

2. Typography & Layout Grids

MEDIUM
Indian Example: Look at a standard "UPSC Coaching" thumbnail. It is text-heavy. Why? Because the audience values information density over minimalism. Compare that to a Cred App ad (minimalist). Design for the audience!

The Grid System

Grids give structure. They stop your design from looking "messy".

[Rule of Thirds Grid] Place focal points at intersections

Typography Basics

  • Serif: Has "feet". Traditional, Serious (Times New Roman). Used in Newspapers.
  • Sans-Serif: No feet. Modern, Clean (Arial, Roboto). Used in Apps.
  • Kerning: Space between specific characters.
  • Leading: Line height (vertical space).

3. Thumbnail Engineering (CTR)

ADVANCED

A thumbnail's only job is to get the click. We use Visual Tension and Curiosity Gaps.

The 3-Second Rule: If a viewer cannot understand your thumbnail in 3 seconds on a mobile screen, it fails.

Elements of High CTR:

  1. Contrast: Bright foreground, dark background (or vice versa).
  2. Emotion: Extreme facial expressions (Shock, Joy, Sadness). humans connect with faces.
  3. Legibility: Bold, Sans-Serif fonts. Max 4-5 words.
  4. Focal Point: Guide the eye using arrows or brightness.

4. Gestalt & UX Psychology

EXPERT

Advanced designers use cognitive bias to direct behavior.

Industry Insight: Why do Netflix thumbnails change? They use A/B testing. If you watch romance movies, the thumbnail shows the couple. If you watch action, it shows the explosion. This is Dynamic Personalization.

Gestalt Principles

  • Proximity: Things close together are seen as a group.
  • Similarity: Things that look alike share function (e.g., all buttons are blue).
  • Closure: The brain fills in missing gaps (e.g., the IBM logo).
  • Continuity: The eye follows lines/curves.

UX Laws

  • Hick's Law: More choices = more time to decide. Keep layouts simple.
  • F-Pattern: How users scan websites (Top left, across, down, across).
