CHAPTER 1: LIVING AND NON-LIVING THINGS

📚 My Notes

1. Definition

Living Things: Things that have life. They breathe, eat, grow, and feel.

Non-Living Things: Things that do not have life. They cannot move or grow on their own.

🖼️ IMAGE: A Dog (Living) vs A Stone (Non-Living)
💡 TIP: If it needs food, it's Living!
❓ Check: Is your pencil living or non-living?
2. Characteristics of Life

All living things share special features: Growth, Movement, Respiration, Nutrition, Excretion, Reproduction, and Feeling.

🖼️ IMAGE: Circle chart of 7 characteristics
💡 TIP: MRS GREN (Movement, Respiration, Sensitivity, Growth, Reproduction, Excretion, Nutrition)
3. Growth

Living things grow in size and shape. A baby becomes an adult. A seed becomes a tree.

Non-living things do not grow (unless we add material to them, like a sand pile).

🖼️ IMAGE: Seed -> Sapling -> Tree
❓ Check: Do your clothes grow?
4. Movement

Animals: Move from place to place (Locomotion) for food and shelter.

Plants: Do not move place, but parts move (e.g., Sunflower turns to sun).

🖼️ IMAGE: Running Deer & Sunflower turning
5. Breathing

Living things need air. Animals use lungs, gills, or skin. Plants use tiny pores called Stomata.

🖼️ IMAGE: Human Lungs vs Leaf Stomata
💡 India Example: Like we take deep breaths in Yoga (Pranayam).
6. Food & Energy

Animals eat plants or other animals. Plants make their own food using sunlight (Photosynthesis).

🖼️ IMAGE: Cow eating grass vs Plant in sun
7. Waste Removal

Living things throw out waste. Animals sweat and urinate. Plants shed old leaves or release gum.

🖼️ IMAGE: Sweating boy & Tree dropping leaves
8. Reproduction

Producing young ones of their own kind. Animals lay eggs or give birth. Plants grow from seeds or stems.

🖼️ IMAGE: Hen with chicks & Plant from seed
9. Life Span

All living things take birth, grow, become old, and finally die. This time period is called Life Span.

🖼️ IMAGE: Timeline: Baby -> Old Man
💡 Fact: Tortoises can live for 100+ years!
10. Response to Stimuli

Reacting to changes around us. We shiver in cold. Plants like 'Touch-me-not' close leaves when touched.

🖼️ IMAGE: Mimosa (Touch-me-not) plant closing
11. Adaptation

Changes in body to survive in an environment. Fish have fins to swim. Cactus has thorns to save water.

🖼️ IMAGE: Camel in desert vs Fish in water
12. Interdependence

Plants and animals depend on each other. Plants give oxygen; Animals give carbon dioxide.

🖼️ IMAGE: Cycle of gas exchange
13. Plants vs Animals

Plants: Make food (Autotrophs), Fixed in place.

Animals: Find food (Heterotrophs), Can move.

14. Classification

Grouping things to study them easily. E.g., Vertebrates (backbone) vs Invertebrates (no backbone).

🖼️ IMAGE: Cat (Vertebrate) vs Worm (Invertebrate)
15. Importance of Life

Living things provide food, medicine, wood, and balance gases in air.

16. Balance in Nature

Predators eat prey to keep numbers in check. If lions disappear, deer will increase and eat all grass.

🖼️ IMAGE: Food chain: Grass -> Deer -> Lion
17. Life Cycle

The stages of development. Butterfly: Egg -> Caterpillar -> Pupa -> Butterfly.

🖼️ IMAGE: Butterfly Life Cycle Chart
18. Being a Scientist

Use your eyes, ears, and nose to observe nature. Note down changes in plants and pets.
