🌱 Class 5 Science: Chapter 2

Plants: Structure, Functions & Processes

📖 Study Notes

1. Types of Plants

Plants are grouped based on their size and stem structure.

  • Herbs: Small plants with soft green stems. (e.g., Mint, Coriander)
  • Shrubs: Medium size, hard woody stems near the ground. (e.g., Rose, Hibiscus)
  • Trees: Tall, strong, woody trunk. (e.g., Banyan, Mango)
  • Climbers: Weak stem, need support to grow up. (e.g., Money Plant)
  • Creepers: Weak stem, spread on the ground. (e.g., Pumpkin)
🇮🇳 Example: In an Indian kitchen garden, Tulsi is a herb, Jasmine is a shrub, and Mango is a tree.

💡 Tip: Herbs are soft, Shrubs are bushy, Trees are tall giants!

2. Parts of a Plant

A plant has two main systems:

  • Root System: Grows below the soil.
  • Shoot System: Grows above the soil (Stem, leaves, flowers, fruits).
[Diagram: Plant Parts Labelled]

Check: Which part fixes the plant to the soil? (Answer: Root)

3. Root System

Roots absorb water and hold the plant firmly.

  • Tap Root: One main thick root with smaller side roots. (e.g., Carrot, Radish, Mustard)
  • Fibrous Root: A bunch of thin roots growing from the base of the stem. (e.g., Grass, Wheat, Rice)
🇮🇳 Example: Pulling out grass is hard because fibrous roots hold soil tightly.
[Diagram: Tap Root vs Fibrous Root]

4. The Stem

The stem supports the plant and carries water/food. It is like a pipeline.

  • Carries water from roots to leaves.
  • Carries food from leaves to other parts.
💡 Tip: Stem = Transport Highway of the plant 🚚

5. The Leaf

Leaves are the "Kitchen of the Plant".

  • Lamina: Flat green part.
  • Veins: Lines on the leaf for transport.
  • Stomata: Tiny holes for breathing.
[Diagram: Leaf Structure & Stomata]

6. Photosynthesis

The process by which green plants make their own food.

Ingredients: Sunlight + Water + Carbon Dioxide + Chlorophyll.

Output: Glucose (Food) + Oxygen (Gas we breathe).

🇮🇳 Example: Like mom needs fire, water, and rice to cook, plants need Sun, water, and CO2.

7. Transportation

How do things move inside a plant?

  • Water Transport (Xylem): Tubes carrying water UP from roots.
  • Food Transport (Phloem): Tubes carrying food DOWN/UP from leaves to everywhere.

8. Transpiration

Loss of excess water from leaves through stomata as water vapor. It helps keep the plant cool.

💡 Tip: Plants "sweat" water to stay cool! 💧

9. Reproduction & Pollination

Flowers turn into fruits. Fruits have seeds.

  • Pollination: Transfer of pollen grains from Stamen (Male) to Stigma (Female) by wind, water, or insects.
  • Fertilization: Joining of male and female parts to form a seed.
🇮🇳 Example: Bees hovering over Marigold flowers in a garden help in pollination.

10. Seed Journey

Germination: A seed growing into a baby plant (seedling). Needs Air, Water, Warmth.

Dispersal: Seeds spreading away from parent plant.

  • Wind: Cotton, Dandelion (Lightweight).
  • Water: Coconut (Floats).
  • Animals: Cocklebur (Hooks stick to fur).
  • Explosion: Peas (Pods burst open).

11. Adaptations

  • Desert (Cactus): No leaves (spines) to save water. Green stem makes food.
  • Aquatic (Lotus): Hollow stem to float. Waxy leaves so they don't rot.
  • Mountain (Pine): Needle-like leaves to slide off snow. Cone shape.
