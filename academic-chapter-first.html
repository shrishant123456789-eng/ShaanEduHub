LABORATORY NOTES: THEORY MODULES

1.1 The Learning Mechanism (Basic)

BASIC
Definition: Learning is a relatively permanent change in behavior or knowledge due to experience. The core process is the Information Processing Model.
Three Stages of Memory:
  1. Encoding: Taking information in (Input). Like typing data into a computer.
  2. Storage: Retaining information (Save). Like the hard drive.
  3. Retrieval: Getting information out (Output). Opening the file.
[Diagram: Eye (Sensory) → Short Term Memory → (Rehearsal) → Long Term Memory]

INDIAN CONTEXT Preparing for UPSC exams involves heavy Encoding (reading), Storage (revision), and Retrieval (writing the exam). Failure often happens at retrieval, not storage.

💡 Tip: Active Recall (testing yourself) strengthens Retrieval better than re-reading.

1.2 Cognitive Load & Attention (Medium)

MEDIUM
Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller): Working memory has limited capacity.
  • Intrinsic Load: Difficulty of the topic itself (e.g., Quantum Physics).
  • Extraneous Load: Bad teaching/distractions (e.g., loud noises, messy slides).
  • Germane Load: Effort used to create permanent schemas (Good load).

INDIAN CONTEXT Studying for JEE while watching IPL creates high Extraneous Load, reducing space for Germane Load.

Attention: It is a limited resource. Selective Attention is the ability to focus on one thing while ignoring others (Cocktail Party Effect).

1.3 Metacognition (Advanced)

ADVANCED
Definition: "Thinking about thinking" (Flavell, 1979). It involves monitoring and controlling your own learning process.
[Diagram: Plan → Monitor → Evaluate Cycle]

INDIAN CONTEXT A student realizing "I am weak in Geometry, I need to spend 2 more hours there" is using Metacognitive Regulation.

1.4 Strategic & Self-Regulated Learning (Expert)

EXPERT
The Interplay: Motivation fuels the engine, but Strategy steers the car. High motivation with poor strategy (rote memorization for analytical exams) leads to failure.
Spacing Effect: Distributed practice yields better long-term retention than massed practice (cramming).
