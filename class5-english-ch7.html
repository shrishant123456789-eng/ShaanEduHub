Chapter 7: Reading Skills & Literature (Advanced)

Prose, Poetry, Drama & Comprehension

MY NOTES

1. Prose (Stories)

Prose is written language in ordinary form. It has paragraphs, not stanzas.

Elements of a Story: Setting (Place), Characters (People), Plot (Beginning, Middle, End), and Theme (Main Idea).

[Image: An open storybook with a castle and a forest]

Indian Example: Panchatantra stories teach us morals like 'Unity is Strength'.

💡 TIP: Think of a Burger! Top bun (Intro), Meat/Veg (Middle Action), Bottom bun (Ending).

Quick Check: What do we call the place where a story happens? (Ans: Setting)

2. Poetry

Poetry uses rhythm and feelings. It looks different from prose.

  • Rhyme: Words with same ending sounds (Cat - Hat).
  • Imagery: Words that create a picture in your mind.
  • Stanza: A group of lines in a poem.
[Image: Clouds shaped like animals - Imagery]
💡 TIP: Rhyme Scheme pattern: AABB means line 1&2 rhyme, line 3&4 rhyme.

3. Drama (Plays)

A story written to be acted out.

Dialogue: Words spoken by characters.

Script: The written text of a play.

[Image: Two masks - Happy and Sad]

Example: Role-playing as a shopkeeper and customer.

4. Character Sketch

Describing a person in the story.

Physical Traits: Tall, blue eyes, curly hair.

Personality Traits: Kind, brave, rude, greedy.

Evidence: We know he is brave because he fought the lion.

💡 TIP: Use Adjectives! Smart, funny, honest.

5. Theme & Inference

Theme: The big message of the story (e.g., Honesty is best).

Inference: Reading between the lines. Using clues to guess what is not written.

Example: If Ram is holding an umbrella and is wet, we infer it was raining.

[Image: Detective magnifying glass looking for clues]

6 & 7. Reading & Listening

Fluency: Reading smoothly without getting stuck.

Expression: Changing voice for sad, happy, or angry lines.

Intonation: Voice going up (?) or down (.).

💡 TIP: Pause at commas. Stop at full stops.

8. Vocabulary

Learning new words helps us understand better.

Context Clues: Guessing the meaning of a hard word by looking at the other words around it.

Example: The room was cluttered with toys, clothes, and papers. (Cluttered means messy).
