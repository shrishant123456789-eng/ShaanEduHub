Topic Wise Notes

1. What is an Adjective?

An adjective is a describing word. It tells us more about a noun (person, place, animal, or thing). It adds "flavor" to our sentences!

Example: Rahul is wearing a blue shirt. (Blue describes the shirt)

🎨 [Image: A colorful garden with labeled items like 'Red Rose', 'Tall Tree']
💡 Memory Tip: Adjectives are "Noun Friends"—they always hang out with nouns to talk about them!
❓ Quick Check: In "The hungry tiger roared," which word is the adjective? (Answer: hungry)

2. Types of Adjectives

Adjectives are not all the same! We use different types for different reasons:

  • Adjective of Quality: Tells about size, shape, or color. (Example: A large box)
  • Adjective of Quantity: Tells "how much." (Example: Give me some water)
  • Adjective of Number: Tells "how many." (Example: I have two pens)
  • Proper Adjective: Formed from proper nouns. (Example: I love Indian food)
🔢 [Image: A tray with 3 apples (Number) and a glass half full (Quantity)]
💡 Memory Tip: Proper Adjectives always start with a Capital Letter!
❓ Quick Check: "Five" is what kind of adjective? (Answer: Adjective of Number)

3. Adjective Order

When we use more than one adjective, we must follow a special order so it sounds natural. We usually go: Opinion → Size → Color → Material.

Example: A beautiful (Opinion), small (Size), pink (Color) dress.

📦 [Image: A big brown wooden box with labels for order]
💡 Memory Tip: Think "O-S-C-M" (Only Super Cats Meow).
❓ Quick Check: Which is correct? "A red big bag" or "A big red bag"? (Answer: A big red bag)

4. What is an Adverb?

An adverb is a word that describes a verb (action). It tells us HOW, WHEN, or WHERE an action is happening.

Example: The tortoise walks slowly. (Slowly tells how he walks)

🐢 [Image: A tortoise walking 'slowly' and a rabbit running 'quickly']
💡 Memory Tip: Most adverbs end with "-ly"!
❓ Quick Check: In "She sang sweetly," what is the adverb? (Answer: sweetly)

5. Types of Adverbs

Adverbs answer four big questions:

  • Manner (How?): He shouted loudly.
  • Time (When?): I will go now.
  • Place (Where?): Come here.
  • Frequency (How often?): I always brush my teeth.
⏰ [Image: A clock (Time), a map (Place), and a megaphone (Manner)]
💡 Memory Tip: Adverbs add "extra info" to the action!
❓ Quick Check: "Often" is an adverb of what? (Answer: Frequency)

6. Forming Sentences with Adjectives & Adverbs

A good sentence uses both! Adjectives describe the people/things, and adverbs describe what they do.

Example: The brave soldier fought boldly.

✍️ [Image: Structure chart: [Adj] + [Noun] + [Verb] + [Adv]]
💡 Memory Tip: Mix and match adjectives and adverbs to make your writing exciting!
❓ Quick Check: Make a sentence using "happy" and "happily".

7. Comparisons (Degrees)

When we compare things, we change the adjective:

  • Positive: Simple form. (Rohan is tall)
  • Comparative: Comparing two things. (Rohan is taller than Ali)
  • Superlative: Comparing many things. (Rohan is the tallest in class)
📈 [Image: Three stairs showing 'Big', 'Bigger', 'Biggest']
💡 Memory Tip: Use "er" for 2 and "est" for all!
❓ Quick Check: What is the superlative of "Good"? (Answer: Best)

8. Sentence Building Rules

A complete sentence must have a Subject (who) and a Predicate (what they are doing). It must start with a Capital Letter and end with a Full Stop (.), Question Mark (?), or Exclamation Mark (!).

Example: Birds (Subject) fly in the sky. (Predicate)

🧱 [Image: Bricks building a wall labeled with Sentence Rules]
💡 Memory Tip: A sentence is like a train—it needs an engine (Subject) and carriages (Predicate).
❓ Quick Check: Is "Running fast" a complete sentence? (Answer: No, it has no Subject)

9. Sentence Combining

We can join two small sentences using conjunctions like "and", "but", and "or" to make them flow better.

Example: I like tea. I like coffee. → I like tea and coffee.

🔗 [Image: Two chains being linked by the word 'BUT']
💡 Memory Tip: Use "and" for same things, "but" for different things!
❓ Quick Check: Combine "It is raining" and "I have an umbrella".

10. Punctuating Compound Sentences

When we join two full sentences with "and", "but", or "or", we usually put a small comma (,) before the joining word.

Example: I wanted to play, but it started to rain.

⚠️ [Image: A giant comma protecting the junction of two sentences]
💡 Memory Tip: The comma gives the sentence a little "breath" before the next part.
❓ Quick Check: Where does the comma go in "I went home and I slept"? (Answer: Before 'and')

🚀 Quick Revision Sheet

• Adjective: Describes a Noun (e.g., Happy boy).

• Adverb: Describes a Verb (e.g., Runs quickly).

  • Adjective Types: Quality (how it is), Quantity (how much), Number (how many), Proper (Indian).
  • Adverb Types: Manner (how), Time (when), Place (where), Frequency (how often).
  • Degrees: Positive (Fast), Comparative (Faster), Superlative (Fastest).
  • Sentence Building: Needs a Subject + Verb. Start with Capital, end with Punctuation.
  • Conjunctions: And, But, Or (use a comma before them in compound sentences).
