Class Notes & Concepts
1. Sentence Structure

A group of words that makes complete sense is called a Sentence.

📝
Example: "Birds fly in the sky." (Complete sense)
Not a sentence: "Fly sky in birds." (No sense)

Types based on Structure:

  • Simple: One Subject + One Verb. (I like mangoes.)
  • Compound: Two simple sentences joined by 'and', 'but', 'or'. (I like mangoes and she likes apples.)
  • Complex: One main idea + one dependent idea. (I will play if it stops raining.)
💡 Tip: FANBOYS (For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So) help spot Compound sentences!
2. Subject & Predicate

Every sentence has two parts.

👤 + 🏃
  • Subject: The person or thing doing the action. (Who/What?)
  • Predicate: The part that tells something about the subject.
"The big red bus (Subject) stopped at the gate (Predicate)."
💡 Tip: Ask "Who?" before the verb to find the Subject.
3. Types of Sentences

There are four main types based on function:

❓ ! .
  1. Assertive (Statement): States a fact. Ends with (.)
    Ex: The sun rises in the east.
  2. Interrogative (Question): Asks something. Ends with (?)
    Ex: Where is my book?
  3. Imperative (Command/Request): Order or advice. Ends with (.)
    Ex: Shut the door. Please help me.
  4. Exclamatory (Emotion): Strong feeling. Ends with (!)
    Ex: What a beautiful flower!
4. Parts of Speech (Revision)
📚

Words are divided into 8 classes:

  • Noun: Naming word (Rahul, Delhi, Table).
  • Pronoun: Replaces noun (He, She, It).
  • Verb: Action word (Run, Sleep, Is).
  • Adjective: Describes noun (Good, Tall, Red).
  • Adverb: Describes verb (Slowly, Quickly).
  • Preposition: Shows position (On, In, Under).
  • Conjunction: Joining word (And, But).
  • Interjection: Sudden feeling (Wow! Oh!).
