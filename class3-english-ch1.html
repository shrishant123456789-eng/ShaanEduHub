Chapter Notes

1. The Alphabet Revision

The English language has 26 letters. We call this set of letters the Alphabet. These letters can be written in two ways.

Capital (Big): A, B, C, D...
Small: a, b, c, d...
💡 Tip: Always use a Capital Letter for names (Rahul, India) and to start a sentence.
[Image: Chart showing Aa to Zz]

2. Vowels and Consonants

The 26 letters are divided into two families:

  • Vowels: There are 5 vowels: A, E, I, O, U.
  • Consonants: The other 21 letters (B, C, D, F...) are consonants.
Example: In the word CAT, 'A' is a vowel. 'C' and 'T' are consonants.
💡 Memory Trick: Think of vowels as the glue that holds words together.

3. Phonics & Blending

Every letter makes a sound. When we mix these sounds, we make words. This is called Blending.

Sound check:
B says 'buh' (Ball)
A says 'aah' (Apple)
T says 'tuh' (Table)
B + A + T = BAT
[Image: Picture of a BAT and phonetic symbols]

4. Rhyming Words

Rhyming words are words that have the same ending sound.

Cat - Hat - Mat
Pen - Hen - Ten
Hop - Pop - Top
💡 Check: Close your eyes and listen. If they sound the same at the end, they rhyme!

5. Silent Letters

Sometimes, a letter is written in a word but we do not say its sound. This is a silent letter.

Knee (We say 'Nee', K is silent)
Write (We say 'Rite', W is silent)
Comb (We say 'Com', B is silent)
