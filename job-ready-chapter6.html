Lecture 6.1: Team Communication & Roles

The RACI Matrix: Killing Ambiguity

Confusion about "who does what" kills productivity in 60% of startups. The global standard to fix this is the RACI Model.

R - Responsible: The "Doer". Who creates the work? (e.g., Developer writing code).
A - Accountable: The "Owner". The one person who signs off. (e.g., Project Manager).
C - Consulted: The "Expert". Two-way communication before action. (e.g., Lead Architect).
I - Informed: The "Spectator". One-way update after action. (e.g., Client Support).

Feedback Cycles & The Conflict Triangle

Great teams don't avoid conflict; they manage it. Use the Conflict Triangle to map issues.

  • Task Conflict: Disagreement on the "What" (Healthy).
  • Process Conflict: Disagreement on the "How" (Manageable).
  • Relationship Conflict: Personal issues (Toxic - Stop immediately).

360-Degree Feedback: In Indian IT firms, feedback isn't just Top-Down. Peers, subordinates, and managers all rate performance to remove bias.

Lecture 6.2: Stakeholder Management

Mapping Your Universe

A stakeholder is anyone impacted by your work. We prioritize them using the Power vs. Interest Grid.

High Power, High Interest: Manage Closely (Key Players).
High Power, Low Interest: Keep Satisfied (Investors).
Low Power, High Interest: Keep Informed (Users).
Low Power, Low Interest: Monitor (General Public).

Escalation & Documentation

When things go wrong, do not hide. Use the Escalation Ladder.

Level 1: Peer-to-Peer Resolution (24 hrs)
Level 2: Team Leads (48 hrs)
Level 3: Directors/Sponsors (Critical Blockers)

The Golden Rule: "If it isn't written down, it didn't happen." Maintain a Decision Log (D-Log) for every client meeting.

Lecture 6.3: Cross-Functional Leadership

Influence Without Authority

As a modern leader or Product Manager, you often lead teams that don't report to you. You need Influence Currency.

  • Expertise Currency: Being the most knowledgeable person.
  • Relationship Currency: Emotional bank account with colleagues.
  • Vision Currency: Inspiring others with the "Why".

The Alignment Pyramid

Before any project kick-off, align on:

  1. Mission: Why are we here?
  2. Values: How do we behave?
  3. Strategy: What is the plan?
  4. Execution: Who does what (RACI)?

Lecture 6.4: Strategic Collaboration (Expert)

Decision Frameworks: RAPID & DACI

Stop "committee decisions". Assign specific roles to speed up execution.

DACI Model:
Driver: Herds the cats.
Approver: Makes the call.
Contributors: Subject matter experts.
Informed: Those told the output.

Persuasion Strategy

To convince stakeholders, use the SCQA Framework:

Situation (Status Quo) -> Complication (The Problem) -> Question (What now?) -> Answer (Your Proposal).

Case 1: Dev vs. Sales Conflict

Sales promised a feature by Friday. Dev says it needs 2 weeks. Tempers are flaring.

Solution: Facilitate a "Trade-off Meeting". Use data, not emotion. Show the "Iron Triangle" (Scope vs Time vs Cost). Option: Release a "Lite" version Friday, full version later.
Case 2: The Silent Stakeholder

A key stakeholder never replies to emails but complains at the final demo.

Solution: Change the channel. Switch from email to 1:1 coffee chats or instant messages. Move them from "Keep Informed" to "Manage Closely" in your grid.
Case 3: Analysis Paralysis

The team has been debating the database choice for 3 weeks.

Solution: Apply RAPID. Assign one "Decider". Set a "Disagree and Commit" deadline (e.g., 48 hours).
