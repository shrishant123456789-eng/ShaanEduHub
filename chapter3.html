🎧 LEVEL 1: STUDIO FUNDAMENTALS

1.1 Signal Flow & Waveforms

Audio isn't magic; it's voltage. A waveform represents air pressure changes over time. The louder the sound, the taller the wave (Amplitude). The higher the pitch, the faster the wave cycles (Frequency).

[DIAGRAM: Sine Wave showing Amplitude (Volume) vs Frequency (Pitch)]
Ideally, recording a podcast in a quiet room creates a clean wave. In India, fan noise or a pressure cooker whistle creates "jagged" interference in your waveform.
🛠 Tool: Audacity / GarageBand - Use 'Zoom' to see these jagged edges.
1.2 Clean vs Dirty Signals (S/N Ratio)

Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) is critical. You want high signal (voice) and low noise (hiss). A "dirty" signal has a high noise floor.

Professional mics need "Phantom Power (+48V)" to boost signal cleanly. Without it, you get a weak signal and lots of static.
1.3 Normalization vs Gain

Gain adds volume (and noise). Normalization mathematically raises the highest peak to a set limit (e.g., -1dB) without changing the dynamic range.

🛠 Rule: Normalize AFTER cleaning noise, not before.

🎚️ LEVEL 2: MIXING & SCULPTING

2.1 EQ (Equalization) Logic

EQ is volume control for specific frequencies.
• Low Cut (High Pass): Remove rumbles (< 80Hz).
• Mud: Cut around 200-400Hz to clear clarity.
• Presence: Boost 3k-5kHz for vocal clarity.

Recording a lecture in a hall? You will have too much "boom" (Low Mids). Use EQ to CUT 300Hz to make the teacher's voice clear.
2.2 Compression (The Glue)

Compression reduces the dynamic range. It makes quiet sounds louder and loud sounds quieter, keeping levels consistent.

  • Threshold: When does it start working?
  • Ratio: How hard does it squash? (4:1 is standard for vocals).
Radio DJs sound "thick" and constant because of heavy compression.
2.3 Noise Reduction (Spectral)

Don't just use a noise gate. Spectral repair removes specific sounds (like a phone ring) without killing the voice.

🛠 Tool: Adobe Audition 'Heal' brush or Izotope RX.

🔊 LEVEL 3: SOUND DESIGN & SPATIAL

3.1 Foley & SFX Layering

Real sounds are fake. The sound of bones breaking in movies is usually celery snapping. This is Foley. Layering (Sub + Impact + Highs) creates "Punch".

[DIAGRAM: Layering Stack - Low Frequency Layer + Textural Layer + Transient Click]
3.2 Automation

Static mixes are boring. Automation is drawing the volume/pan moves over time. Ride the fader on vocals to keep them on top of the music.

🏆 LEVEL 4: MASTERING & BROADCAST

4.1 LUFS & Loudness Standards

You cannot just "make it loud". Platforms have rules.
• Spotify: -14 LUFS
• YouTube: -14 LUFS
• Broadcast (TV): -23 or -24 LUFS

If you upload a -8 LUFS (very loud) track to YouTube, YouTube will forcibly turn it down, making it sound weak compared to others.
4.2 The Master Bus Chain

EQ (Polish) -> Multiband Compression (Glue) -> Saturation (Warmth) -> Limiter (Safety ceiling at -0.1dB).
