Chapter Notes 📖

1. Meaning of Living Things

Living things are things that have life in them. They are alive like you and me!

🌟 Example: A boy, a dog, a rose plant, a butterfly.
💡 Tip: If it grows and breathes, it is Living!
🖼️ [Image: A happy child playing with a puppy]
❓ Check: Is your teddy bear living? (No)

2. Meaning of Non-Living Things

Non-living things do not have life. They cannot move or grow on their own.

🌟 Example: Chair, table, pencil, car, stone.
💡 Tip: If it stays the same size forever, it is usually Non-Living.
🖼️ [Image: A table and a chair]
❓ Check: Does a stone feel pain? (No)

3. Characteristics of Living Things

Living things share special features:

  • Growth: A baby grows into a man. A seed grows into a plant.
  • Movement: Animals walk. Plants turn towards the sun.
  • Breathing: We need air to live.
  • Eating: All living things need food.
  • Reproduction: They have babies (young ones).
🖼️ [Image: A seed growing into a tree]

4. Differences between Living and Non-Living

Let's compare them:

  • Living things grow. Non-living things do not grow.
  • Living things feel changes. Non-living things do not feel.
  • Living things die. Non-living things break but don't die.
💡 Tip: You grow, your toy car does not!

5. Examples from Surroundings

Look around your house or school!

Living: Ants, housefly, pet cat, money plant, your teacher.

Non-Living: School bag, water bottle, blackboard, computer.

❓ Check: Is a computer living? (No, it works on electricity)

6. Living Things Need Food, Air and Water

Without food, water, and air, living things cannot stay alive.

  • Food: Gives energy. Plants make their own food!
  • Air: Humans breathe oxygen. Plants take in carbon dioxide.
  • Water: Every living body needs water.
🖼️ [Image: A cow drinking water]

7. Lifespan of Living Beings

Living things are born, they grow, live for some time, and then they die. This time is called a Lifespan.

🌟 Example: A housefly lives for few weeks. A tortoise lives for 100 years!

8. Changes in Non-Living Things

Non-living things change only if we change them or weather changes them.

  • A pencil gets shorter when you sharpen it. It doesn't shrink on its own.
  • Iron rusts due to water and air.
  • Clothes get old and tear.
💡 Tip: They change from outside, not inside.

9. Sorting Living and Non-Living Objects

We can sort things into groups.

Group A (Living): Fish, Tree, Human.

Group B (Non-Living): Book, Pen, Sand.

❓ Check: Where does a 'Cloud' go? (Non-living, even if it moves!)
