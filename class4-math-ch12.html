1. Pictographs (Picture Graphs)

Definition: A Pictograph uses pictures or symbols to represent data information. It makes reading numbers fun!

[Image: A pictograph showing apples. 1 Apple picture = 5 apples]
How to Read:
  • Look at the Key. The key tells you what one picture stands for.
  • Count the pictures.
  • Multiply the count by the value in the key.
Example: If 1 Smile = 10 Students.
Class A has 3 Smiles.
Total Students = 3 × 10 = 30 Students.
💡 Tip: Always check the KEY first! Sometimes 1 picture is not just 1 item.
❓ Check: If 1 Tree = 2 Trees, and you see 4 Trees drawn, how many real trees are there? (Ans: 8)
2. Indian Currency (Money)

Definition: The money used in India is Rupees (₹) and Paise (p).

Notes and Coins:
  • Coins: 50p, ₹1, ₹2, ₹5, ₹10, ₹20.
  • Notes: ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100, ₹200, ₹500.
Important Conversion:
₹1 = 100 paise
To change Rupees to Paise → Multiply by 100.
To change Paise to Rupees → Put a dot (.) before the last 2 digits.
💡 Tip: The dot (.) separates Rupees and Paise. ₹ 15.50 means 15 Rupees and 50 Paise.
❓ Check: How many paise in ₹5? (Ans: 500p)
3. Adding & Subtracting Money

We add and subtract money just like normal numbers. The secret is the DOT!

Rules:
  • Write numbers in columns.
  • Align the dots (One dot exactly below the other).
  • Add or Subtract normally.
  • Put the dot in the answer exactly below the other dots.
Add: ₹ 25.50 + ₹ 10.00
25.50
+ 10.00
-------
35.50
Answer: ₹ 35.50
❓ Check: ₹ 100.00 - ₹ 20.00 = ? (Ans: ₹ 80.00)
4. Bar Graphs (Advanced)

Definition: A Bar Graph uses rectangular bars to show data. Longer bar means bigger number.

[Image: A bar graph showing favorite fruits]
Parts of a Bar Graph:
  • Title: What the graph is about.
  • X-Axis (Bottom line): Shows items (e.g., Apple, Banana).
  • Y-Axis (Side line): Shows numbers/scale (e.g., 0, 5, 10, 15).
  • Scale: The gap between numbers (e.g., Scale: 1 unit = 5 fruits).
💡 Tip: Use a ruler to trace the top of the bar to the number on the side to read it correctly.
5. Pie Charts (Circle Graphs)

Definition: A Pie Chart is a circle divided into slices. The whole circle represents the Total.

[Image: A pie chart divided into half and quarters]
  • The bigger the slice, the bigger the value.
  • Full Circle = 1 Whole.
  • Half Circle = 1/2.
  • Quarter Circle = 1/4.
If a pie chart shows half the class likes Cricket, it means 1/2 of the students like Cricket.
6. Multiplication & Division of Money

Used when buying many items or sharing costs.

Multiplication:

If 1 chocolate costs ₹10, cost of 5 chocolates = 10 × 5 = ₹50.

Division:

If 5 pens cost ₹50, cost of 1 pen = 50 ÷ 5 = ₹10.

💡 Tip: Ignore the dot, calculate, then put the dot back (2 places from right) for paise.
7. Making Bills

A Bill is a statement of money we pay for things we buy.

A Bill has columns for:
  • Item: Name of thing.
  • Quantity: How many?
  • Rate: Price of ONE item.
  • Amount: Quantity × Rate.
Pen | Qty: 2 | Rate: ₹5 | Amount: ₹10 (2×5)
Book| Qty: 1 | Rate: ₹20| Amount: ₹20 (1×20)
Total: ₹30
8. Profit and Loss

Shopkeepers buy things and sell them.

  • Cost Price (CP): Money paid to BUY an item.
  • Selling Price (SP): Money received by SELLING an item.
Formulas:
  • Profit (Gain): When SP > CP.
    Formula: Profit = SP - CP
  • Loss: When CP > SP.
    Formula: Loss = CP - SP
Bought pen for ₹10 (CP). Sold for ₹12 (SP).
SP is bigger. So, Profit.
Profit = 12 - 10 = ₹2.
