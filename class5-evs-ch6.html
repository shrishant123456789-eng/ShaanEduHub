Chapter 6
Matter and Materials

📚 Study Notes

1. Definition of Matter

Matter is anything that has mass (weight) and occupies space (volume).

Everything around us is made of tiny particles called molecules.

🏠 Examples: Books, water, air, chair, pen.
[Image: Balance scale showing mass and a box occupying space]
💡 Tip: If you can touch it or keep it in a jar (even air!), it is Matter.

Quick Check: Is the feeling of happiness matter? No, because it has no mass.

2. States of Matter

Matter exists in three states based on how tightly particles are packed.

🧱 Solids

Particles are tightly packed. They cannot move freely.

  • Fixed Shape: Yes
  • Fixed Volume: Yes
  • Can flow? No
Examples: Brick, Ice, Wood, Iron, Spoon.

💧 Liquids

Particles are loosely packed. They can slide over each other.

  • Fixed Shape: No (takes shape of container)
  • Fixed Volume: Yes
  • Can flow? Yes
Examples: Water, Milk, Juice, Oil.

🎈 Gases

Particles are very far apart. They move very fast in all directions.

  • Fixed Shape: No
  • Fixed Volume: No (fills entire space)
  • Can flow? Yes
Examples: Air, Steam, Oxygen, Perfume smell.
[Image: Diagram showing particle arrangement in Solid, Liquid, Gas]
3. Change of State

Matter can change from one form to another on heating or cooling.

  • Melting: Solid ➡️ Liquid (Heating)
    Ex: Ice melting.
  • Evaporation: Liquid ➡️ Gas (Heating)
    Ex: Boiling water creates steam.
  • Condensation: Gas ➡️ Liquid (Cooling)
    Ex: Water drops on a cold glass.
  • Freezing: Liquid ➡️ Solid (Cooling)
    Ex: Making ice cubes.
🔥 Heat expands (Melting/Evap). ❄️ Cool contracts (Freezing/Cond).
4. Types of Changes

Physical Change: Only the state or shape changes. No new substance is formed. Usually reversible.
Ex: Tearing paper, Melting wax.

Chemical Change: A new substance is formed. Usually irreversible.
Ex: Burning wood, Cooking food, Rusting iron.

Reversible Change: Can go back to original form. (Ice ↔️ Water)

Irreversible Change: Cannot go back. (Milk ➡️ Curd)

[Image: Ice melting vs Paper burning]
5. Solutions and Solubility

Solute: The solid that dissolves (e.g., Salt).

Solvent: The liquid that dissolves the solid (e.g., Water).

Solution: The mixture of solute and solvent (e.g., Salty water).

Soluble Substances: Dissolve in water (Sugar, Salt, Lemon juice).

Insoluble Substances: Do not dissolve (Sand, Chalk powder, Oil).

🥣 Solute (Salt) + Solvent (Water) = Solution.
6. Separation Techniques

How to separate insoluble substances?

  • Sedimentation: Letting impurities settle down.
  • Decantation: Pouring out the clear liquid carefully.
  • Filtration: Using a filter (like a tea strainer or filter paper) to separate solid from liquid.

How to separate soluble substances?

  • Evaporation: Heating the solution so water turns to steam, leaving the solid (salt) behind.
[Image: Funnel with filter paper showing Filtration]
7. Materials in Daily Life

Natural Materials: Found in nature.
Ex: Wood, Cotton, Wool, Gold, Clay.

Man-made Materials: Made by humans in factories.
Ex: Plastic, Nylon, Glass, Steel, Paper.

Transparency Classification:

  • Transparent: Allows light to pass fully. We can see clearly. (Glass, Clear water).
  • Translucent: Allows some light. Vision is blurry. (Butter paper, Frosted glass).
  • Opaque: No light passes. Cannot see through. (Wood, Wall, Metal).

Environment Impact:

We must use materials wisely. Plastics are harmful because they don't rot.

♻️ The 3 Rs: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle.

⭐ Important Questions

  • Q1. Why do solids have a fixed shape?
    Ans: Because the molecules in solids are very tightly packed and cannot move around freely.
  • Q2. Define Evaporation.
    Ans: The process of changing a liquid into a gas on heating is called evaporation.
  • Q3. Give two examples of chemical changes.
    Ans: Burning of paper and cooking of food.
  • Q4. What is a saturated solution?
    Ans: A solution in which no more solute can be dissolved at a given temperature.
  • Q5. Difference between miscible and immiscible liquids?
    Ans: Miscible liquids mix (Milk+Water). Immiscible liquids do not mix (Oil+Water).
  • Q6. How is salt obtained from sea water?
    Ans: By the process of evaporation. Water evaporates in the sun, leaving salt behind.
  • Q7. What is condensation?
    Ans: The process of gas changing into liquid on cooling (e.g., dew drops).
  • Q8. Why is air considered matter?
    Ans: Because air has weight (mass) and occupies space (fills balloons/tyres).
  • Q9. What are translucent objects?
    Ans: Objects that allow only some light to pass through them (e.g., butter paper).
  • Q10. Is rusting of iron reversible?
    Ans: No, rusting is an irreversible chemical change.
  • Q11. Define Solvent.
    The liquid in which a solute dissolves.
  • Q12. What is Filtration?
    Separating insoluble solids from liquids using a filter.
  • Q13. Is breaking glass physical or chemical?
    Physical change.
  • Q14. Name a universal solvent.
    Water.
  • Q15. Molecular arrangement in Gas?
    Very loose, large spaces between particles.
  • Q16. Example of opaque material?
    Wood or Stone.
  • Q17. What is Sedimentation?
    Settling down of heavy insoluble impurities.
  • Q18. Man-made material from trees?
    Paper.
  • Q19. Freezing point of water?
    0 degree Celsius.
  • Q20. Boiling point of water?
    100 degree Celsius.
  • Q21. Is burning a candle reversible?
    No, the wax burns away (Chemical). Melting wax is physical.
  • Q22. What is decantation?
    Pouring out clear liquid after sedimentation.
  • Q23. Define Volume.
    The amount of space an object takes up.
  • Q24. Can gases flow?
    Yes, they flow easily in all directions.
  • Q25. What happens to molecules on heating?
    They move faster and spread apart.
  • Q26. Example of sublimation?
    Camphor (Kapoor) turns directly from solid to gas.
  • Q27. Is sugar soluble?
    Yes, sugar dissolves completely in water.
  • Q28. Why use plastic for electric wires?
    Plastic is an insulator (does not conduct electricity).
  • Q29. What is an alloy?
    A mixture of two metals (e.g., Brass).
  • Q30. 3 States of water?
    Ice (Solid), Water (Liquid), Water Vapour (Gas).
