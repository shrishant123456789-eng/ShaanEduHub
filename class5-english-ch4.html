1. Types of Adjectives

An Adjective describes a Noun.

🔴 🔢 ❓
  • Quality: Good, Bad, Red (Descriptive).
  • Quantity: Some, Little, Much (Uncountable).
  • Number: One, First, Many (Countable).
  • Demonstrative: This, That, These, Those (Pointing).
  • Possessive: My, Your, His, Our (Ownership).
  • Interrogative: Which? What? Whose? (Questioning).
Example: That (Dem.) red (Qual.) car is mine.
💡 Tip: Ask "What kind?" for Quality and "How much?" for Quantity.
2. Degrees of Comparison

When we compare things, adjectives change form.

🐁 🐕 🐘
  1. Positive: Big (1 thing).
  2. Comparative: Bigger (2 things). Use 'than'.
  3. Superlative: Biggest (3+ things). Use 'the'.
Example: Ravi is taller than Raj. Amit is the tallest.
3. Adjective Order

If many adjectives come together, follow this order:

📋 ➡️ 📦

OSASCOMP

  • Opinion (Beautiful)
  • Size (Small)
  • Age (Old)
  • Shape (Round)
  • Colour (Red)
  • Origin (Indian)
  • Material (Wooden)
  • Purpose (Walking) stick
💡 Memorize: O-SAS-COMP
4. Adverbs

An Adverb adds meaning to a Verb, Adjective, or another Adverb.

🏃💨
  • Manner: How? (Slowly, Fast, Bravely).
  • Place: Where? (Here, There, Up).
  • Time: When? (Now, Yesterday, Soon).
  • Frequency: How often? (Always, Never, Often).
Check: He runs fast. (Manner)
5. Adjective vs Adverb
🤔 🆚 🧐

Adjective: Describes Noun. (He is a quiet boy.)

Adverb: Describes Verb. (He sits quietly.)

💡 Most adverbs end in -ly, but not all (e.g., Fast, Hard)!
6. Usage & Speaking

Use strong adjectives to improve descriptions.

Weak: Very good -> Strong: Excellent
Weak: Very big -> Strong: Huge

When describing, use: It looks... It feels... It seems...
