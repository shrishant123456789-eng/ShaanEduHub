MY CLASS NOTES

1. Coordinating Conjunctions

These words join two equal parts of a sentence. They join words, phrases, or simple sentences.

💡 TIP: Remember FANBOYS
  • For (reason)
  • And (addition)
  • Nor (not either)
  • But (contrast)
  • Or (choice)
  • Yet (contrast)
  • So (result)
Example: I wanted to play cricket, but it started raining.
[Image: A boy holding a bat AND a ball]

Quick Check: Which word joins two opposite ideas? (Ans: But)

2. Subordinating Conjunctions

These words join a main sentence with a dependent clause (a part that cannot stand alone).

Common words: because, although, if, when, while, since.

Example: Although he was tired, he finished his homework.
💡 TIP: These usually show 'Time', 'Reason', or 'Condition'.
[Image: Clock showing 'When', Cloud showing 'Because']

3. Joining Sentences Logically

We must use the correct conjunction to make sense. Do not write run-on sentences (sentences that go on without stopping).

Wrong: I went to the market I bought milk.
Correct: I went to the market and bought milk.

Key Rule: If the sentence starts with a subordinating conjunction, use a comma in the middle.

4. Interjections

Short words that show sudden strong emotion. They are not grammatically connected to the rest of the sentence.

  • Wow! (Surprise)
  • Ouch! (Pain)
  • Hurray! (Joy)
  • Alas! (Sadness)
Example: Ouch! I hurt my toe.
[Image: Emoji faces - Happy, Sad, Surprised]

5. Punctuation Marks

Traffic signals for reading!

  • Full Stop (.): End of a telling sentence.
  • Comma (,): Short pause or list separator.
  • Question Mark (?): Asking something.
  • Exclamation Mark (!): Strong feeling.
  • Apostrophe ('): Shows possession (Ravi's bag) or contraction (Can't).
Example: Are you hungry? No, I am not.

6 & 7. Expression & Listening

When reading aloud, pause at commas and stop at full stops. Raise your voice at question marks.

💡 TIP: Comma = Count 1. Full stop = Count 2.
"Wait! Don't go there." (Read with energy!)

Quick Recap

  • FANBOYS: For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So (Coordinating).
  • Subordinating: Because, if, although, when (Needs main clause).
  • Interjections: Emotion words (Wow! Oh!). Use (!) sign.
  • Punctuation: Capital letters start sentences. Full stops end them. Commas pause.
  • Apostrophe: Possession (Tom's cat) or Contraction (It's).
