Study Notes

1. Alphabet Revision & Correct Sounds

Each of the 26 letters in English has a special name and a special sound (phonics).

  • A says 'Ah' (Apple)
  • B says 'Buh' (Ball)
  • C says 'Cuh' (Cat)
[Image: Chart of 26 letters with animal pictures]

Example: The letter 'S' sounds like a snake's hiss – 'Sss'.

Memory Tip: Think of the sound an object makes to remember the letter sound!

Quick Check: What sound does the letter 'D' make?

2. Phonetic Sounds (Short & Long Vowels)

Vowels (A, E, I, O, U) have two main sounds.

  • Short Vowel: Fast sound. Ex: 'Cat' (A), 'Bed' (E).
  • Long Vowel: Says its own name. Ex: 'Cake' (A), 'Sleep' (E).
[Image: Comparison table of Short vs Long vowel sounds]

Example: In 'Bit', 'i' is short. In 'Bite', 'i' is long.

Memory Tip: Usually, if a word ends in 'e', the vowel before it becomes long!

Quick Check: Is the 'o' in 'Rope' long or short?

3. Consonant Blends (pl, br, tr, cr, dr, str)

A blend is when two or three consonants come together, but you can still hear each sound quickly.

  • br + ead = Bread
  • tr + ee = Tree
  • str + aw = Straw
[Image: Drawing of a Tree, Bread, and Straw with blends highlighted]

Example: 'Pl' in 'Play' is a blend of P and L.

Memory Tip: Say them fast like a slide!

Quick Check: Name a word starting with 'Cr'.

4. Diphthongs (ai, ea, oi, ou)

Diphthongs are "gliding sounds" where two vowels together make one unique sound.

  • ai: Rain, Pain
  • ea: Tea, Leaf
  • oi: Coin, Boil
  • ou: Cloud, House
[Image: Cloud with 'ou' written inside, Coin with 'oi']

Example: In 'Cloud', the 'o' and 'u' join to make an 'ow' sound.

Memory Tip: Think of two vowels holding hands to make a new sound!

Quick Check: Which diphthong is in 'Leaf'?

5. Syllable Division

Syllables are the "beats" in a word. Every syllable must have a vowel sound.

  • Cat (1 beat)
  • Wa-ter (2 beats)
  • E-le-phant (3 beats)
[Image: Hands clapping for beats in words]

Example: 'Teacher' has two syllables: Teach-er.

Memory Tip: Put your hand under your chin. Every time your chin drops, that's a syllable!

Quick Check: How many syllables are in 'Banana'?

6. Word Families

Words that have the same ending pattern belong to a family. They usually rhyme!

  • -at family: Cat, Mat, Hat, Bat
  • -ing family: Sing, Wing, King
[Image: A house labeled '-at' with Cat and Hat inside]

Example: 'Light' and 'Night' are in the '-ight' family.

Memory Tip: Changing only the first letter creates a new family member!

Quick Check: Give a word from the '-op' family.

7. Compound Words

When two small words join together to make a brand new word with a new meaning.

  • Rain + Bow = Rainbow
  • Sun + Flower = Sunflower
  • Foot + Ball = Football
[Image: Illustration of Sun + Flower = Sunflower]

Example: 'Key' and 'Board' make 'Keyboard'.

Memory Tip: Word + Word = New Magic Word!

Quick Check: What do you get if you join 'Bed' and 'Room'?

8. Prefixes & Suffixes (Basic)

Prefixes: Added to the start. Ex: Unhappy (Not happy).

Suffixes: Added to the end. Ex: Helpful (Full of help).

[Image: A train with 'Prefix' as engine and 'Suffix' as tail]

Example: 'Re' + 'play' = Replay (Play again).

Memory Tip: 'Pre' means 'before'. Like a preview!

Quick Check: Add a suffix to 'Kind' to make it an adverb.

9. Homophones (Basic)

Words that sound the same but have different spellings and meanings.

  • Sun (in the sky) / Son (boy child)
  • See (look) / Sea (ocean)
  • Two (number) / To (direction)
[Image: Drawing of an Eye (See) vs Waves (Sea)]

Example: I ate eight apples.

Memory Tip: 'Homo' means same, 'Phone' means sound!

Quick Check: Which homophone means a story? (Tail / Tale)

10. Spelling Rules (ie/ei, double letters)

Spelling can be tricky, but rules help!

  • Rule 1: 'i' before 'e', except after 'c' (Believe, but Receive).
  • Rule 2: Double the consonant if the vowel is short (Hop → Hopping).
[Image: Rule chart for 'i' before 'e']

Example: 'Friend' follows 'i before e'.

Memory Tip: Chant the 'i before e' poem!

Quick Check: Is it 'Relieve' or 'Releive'?

11. Dictionary Usage (Introduction)

A dictionary helps us find the meaning and spelling of words. Words are listed in Alphabetical Order (A-Z).

  • Apple comes before Banana.
  • Cat comes before Cup (look at the 2nd letter!).
[Image: Open dictionary with guide words highlighted]

Example: To find 'Zebra', look at the very end of the book.

Memory Tip: The guide words at the top tell you the first and last word on that page!

Quick Check: Which comes first: 'Dog' or 'Deer'?

12. Word Pronunciation Practice

Speaking clearly is important. Practice these hard words:

  • Knowledge: (Nol-edge) - The 'K' is silent!
  • Recipe: (Res-i-pee)
  • Island: (Eye-land) - The 'S' is silent!
[Image: Mouth showing correct tongue position for 'th' sound]

Example: In 'Knife', we don't say the 'K'.

Memory Tip: Listen carefully and repeat three times!

Quick Check: Is the 'W' in 'Write' silent or spoken?
