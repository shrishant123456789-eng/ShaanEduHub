1.1 Professional Communication Fundamentals

What is Professional Communication?

It is the clear, accurate, and ethical exchange of information within a business context. Unlike casual chat, every professional interaction has a specific goal (to inform, persuade, or instruct).

Tone: Formal vs. Informal

Feature Formal (Professional) Informal (Casual)
Context Reports, Official Emails, Clients Team Chat, Lunch breaks
Language Complete sentences, no slang Abbreviations, emojis, slang
Example "Could you please update the file?" "Pls fix file asap thx"

Email Etiquette Essentials

  • Subject Line: Must be specific (e.g., "Invoice #402 Issue" vs "Hello").
  • Greetings: "Dear [Name]," or "Hi [Name]," (Avoid "Hey guys").
  • Closings: "Best regards," "Sincerely," or "Thanks,".
  • Tone Check: Never write an email when angry. Use neutral words.
❌ Bad Example: "I need the report now. You are late."
✅ Good Example: "Could you please provide an update on the report status? We need to finalize the presentation by 2 PM."

1.2 Workplace Communication Systems

Documentation Formats

Companies run on documentation. If it isn't written down, it didn't happen.

1. The Memo (Memorandum)

Used for internal announcements (policy changes, promotions).
Format: To: All Staff | From: HR | Date: Oct 12 | Subject: New Holiday Policy.

2. Meeting Minutes

The official record of a meeting. Must include:

  • Date, Time, Attendees.
  • Key discussion points.
  • Action Items: Who does what by when?

Version Control & Collaboration

When working on shared docs (Google Docs, SharePoint), never overwrite someone's work without tracking changes. Use comments for suggestions instead of deleting text directly.

1.3 Organizational Structure & Culture

Understanding Hierarchy

Knowing who reports to whom prevents communication breakdown.

CEO / Director
Department Manager
Team Lead A
Team Lead B
You (Junior Exec)

Corporate Culture

Culture is "how things are done here."

  • Hierarchical Culture: Strict protocols, formal titles (Mr./Ms.).
  • Clan Culture: Family-like, informal, collaborative.
  • Adhocracy: Innovative, risk-taking (Startups).

Tip: Observe your team for the first week. Do they use email or Slack? Do they leave at 5 PM sharp or stay late?

1.4 Professional Ethics & Responsibility

The Ethical Decision Framework

  1. Identify the issue: Is this legal? Is it fair?
  2. Gather facts: Don't assume. Check policies.
  3. Evaluate options: What happens if I do X vs Y?
  4. Act & Reflect: Make the decision that aligns with integrity.

Key Areas of Responsibility

  • Confidentiality: Never share client data or passwords.
  • Accountability: Own your mistakes immediately. "I missed the deadline, here is my plan to fix it."
  • Digital Footprint: Work devices are monitored. Avoid personal browsing.
