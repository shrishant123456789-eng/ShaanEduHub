Class Notes 📊
1. Collection of Data

Data is a collection of facts, numbers, or information. When we ask people about their favorite fruit or count cars on a road, we are collecting data.

💡 TIP: Raw data is mixed up. We need to organize it!
Check: Is a list of student marks called data? (Yes)
2. Organizing Data

After collecting data, we arrange it to understand it better. We can organize data in Ascending (small to big) or Descending (big to small) order.

💡 TIP: Organized data is easier to read.
3. Tally Marks

Tally marks are a quick way to count. We draw vertical lines for 1, 2, 3, 4. For 5, we draw a diagonal line across the four. This makes a group of 5.

💡 TIP: Count in 5s (5, 10, 15...) using tally groups.
Check: How do you write 6 in tally? (One group of 5 + 1 line)
4. Frequency Tables

A table that shows 'how many times' an item appears. 'Frequency' means the count.

💡 TIP: Tally marks help fill the Frequency Table.
5. Pictographs

Representing data using pictures. ALWAYS check the **Key**. One picture might stand for 10 items!

💡 TIP: If 1 face = 10 kids, then half face = 5 kids.
6. Bar Graphs

Using rectangular bars to show numbers. Taller bar means more value.
• X-axis: Horizontal line (Names)
• Y-axis: Vertical line (Numbers/Scale)

💡 TIP: The width of bars must be same. Gaps must be equal.
7. Reading Graphs

To read a bar graph, look at the top of the bar and trace it to the number on the vertical axis.

Check: If bar touches line 50, value is? (50)
8. Comparison Using Data

Graphs help us compare quickly.
• Highest bar = Maximum value
• Shortest bar = Minimum value
• Equal height = Same value

9. Mean (Average - Basic)

Mean is the central value.
Formula: Sum of all values ÷ Number of values.
Example: Average of 2 and 4 is (2+4)÷2 = 3.

💡 TIP: Add them all up, divide by how many there are.
10. Real-life Data

We use data handling for Census (counting people), Weather reports (temperature), Cricket scores, and Exam results.

11. Logical Interpretation

Using data to predict or understand trends. E.g., If ice cream sales are high in May, it means May is hot.

💡 TIP: Look at the pattern, not just numbers.
