Module 1: Foundations of Research

🟢 BASIC

1. What Research Means

Definition: Research is not just "Googling". It is a systematic inquiry to describe, explain, predict, and control the observed phenomenon.

It involves inductive and deductive methods. In higher education, research moves from "consuming knowledge" to "creating knowledge".

🇮🇳 Indian Context Example: A student isn't just asking "Why is UPI popular?". They are surveying 500 shopkeepers in rural Bihar to quantify the shift from cash to digital wallets.
💡 Memory Tip: Research = Question + Evidence + Conclusion
[DIAGRAM: Cycle of Research]
Observation ➔ Question ➔ Hypothesis ➔ Experiment ➔ Analysis ➔ Conclusion
🟢 BASIC

2. Identifying Keywords & Boolean Logic

Search engines are dumb. They need specific commands. A search string is your primary tool.

Topic: "Impact of social media on youth productivity"
Keywords: Social Media, Youth, Productivity, Academic Performance.
Boolean Operators:
  • AND: Narrows (Youth AND Productivity)
  • OR: Broadens (Instagram OR Facebook)
  • NOT: Excludes (Virus NOT Computer)

🟡 MEDIUM

3. Source Evaluation (The CRAAP Test)

Never trust a source blindly. Use the CRAAP test for every academic paper or article.
  • Currency: Is it up to date?
  • Relevance: Does it answer your specific question?
  • Authority: Is the author a qualified scholar?
  • Accuracy: Is it supported by evidence?
  • Purpose: Is it objective or trying to sell something?

4. Annotation & Active Reading

Passive reading is useless for research. You must engage with the text.

Techniques:
• SQ3R: Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review.
• Cornell Notes: Split page into Cues (Left), Notes (Right), Summary (Bottom).
• Syntopical Reading: Reading 5 papers on the same topic simultaneously to find patterns.

5. Synthesizing Evidence

Synthesis is not a list of summaries. It is combining parts to form a new whole.

Bad: Author A says X. Author B says Y.
Good: While Author A argues X, Author B contradicts this by demonstrating Y, suggesting that the context of Z is critical.

6. Argument Building & Fallacies

An academic argument consists of three pillars: Claim (What you believe), Evidence (Data), and Warrant (Logic connecting them).

Common Fallacies to Avoid:
  • Ad Hominem: Attacking the person, not the argument.
  • Strawman: Misrepresenting an opponent's view to defeat it easily.
  • Confirmation Bias: Ignoring evidence that disproves your theory.
[CHART: The Toulmin Model of Argumentation]
Data ➔ Warrant ➔ Backing ➔ Qualifier ➔ Rebuttal ➔ Claim
🟣 EXPERT

7. Independent Research Workflow

From idea to paper, the path is rigorous.

  1. Problem Statement: What gap are you filling?
  2. Literature Review: What is already known?
  3. Methodology: Qualitative (Interviews) or Quantitative (Stats)?
  4. Data Collection & Analysis.
  5. Discussion: Interpreting results.
[FLOWCHART: Research Pipeline]
Concept ➔ Ethics Approval ➔ Data Collection ➔ Analysis ➔ Peer Review
🟣 EXPERT

8. Publish-Ready Thinking

Academic writing is formal, objective, and precise. It avoids "I think" and "In my opinion".

Citation Styles:
• APA: Psychology, Education, Sciences.
• MLA: Humanities, Literature.
• Chicago: History, Business.
Plagiarism is the cardinal sin. Always cite sources.
🟣 EXPERT

9. Logic Frameworks

Senior academic writing relies on robust logic structures.

  • Deductive: General theory ➔ Specific confirmation (All men are mortal ➔ Socrates is a man ➔ Socrates is mortal).
  • Inductive: Specific observation ➔ General theory (I see 100 white swans ➔ All swans are white). Note: Inductive is probabilistic, not certain.

Summary & Revision

Key Takeaways

  • Research is systematic, not random.
  • Keywords and Boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) refine searches.
  • CRAAP Test (Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose) evaluates sources.
  • Synthesis combines multiple sources into a new argument.
  • Fallacies like Ad Hominem and Strawman weaken arguments.
  • Deductive Logic moves from General to Specific.
  • Inductive Logic moves from Specific to General.
  • Citation (APA, MLA) prevents plagiarism and builds authority.
